# ORVIA Story-Driven Website Template Standard

## Core rule

A website is not designed from a page list first.

It is designed from the story:

**Who are you? → Why do you exist? → What problem are you trying to solve? → What happens if it is ignored? → What have you learned? → What do you offer? → What can you prove? → What should the visitor do next?**

The finished website is the visual storyboard of that answer.

## One template, two modes

### 1. ORVIA estate mode

Used for ORVIA Oversight and every ORVIA system.

The template automatically provides:
- ORVIA Oversight parent identity
- active system icon + name
- estate navigation
- shared header/footer
- trust/legal registry
- accessibility rules
- O/R/V/I/A method treatment
- standard CTA system
- IRIS/customer handoff slots
- approved media slots
- case-study, toolkit, insights and social modules
- mobile behaviour
- analytics / lead attribution hooks

The brief does **not** rebuild those items. It only populates them.

### 2. ORVIA Web client mode

Used for paying website-builder customers.

The same story engine and section library are used, but the client receives their own:
- identity
- logo
- palette
- navigation
- story
- services
- proof
- commercial routes
- legal details

ORVIA's internal brand must not visually overwhelm or masquerade as the client's brand.

## Intake before design

Every site brief must answer:

### Story
- What is the one-sentence story?
- Why does the organisation exist?
- What happened that made this necessary?
- What problem does it solve?
- What is at stake if nothing changes?
- What has it learned that others may not see?
- What has it built / changed / created?
- What does success look like?
- Why should a human care?

### Audience
- Who is this for?
- What are they worried about?
- What do they need to understand?
- What do they need to do next?

### Boundaries
- Must have
- Must not have
- Claims requiring proof
- Legal / regulatory boundaries
- Tone boundaries
- Privacy / consent boundaries

### Commercial
- What can be bought immediately?
- What requires discovery / proposal?
- What is free?
- What happens after a CTA is clicked?
- Who owns the lead?
- What is the expected response time?
- Where does onboarding begin?

### Proof
- What is verified?
- What case studies are publishable?
- What testimonials are approved?
- What numbers can be evidenced?
- What trust signals are relevant?

### Media
- Hero image or film
- Flagship explainer
- supporting cuts
- founder/owner message where relevant
- approved service imagery
- icons / diagrams / screenshots

## Storyboard rule

The template converts the brief into this default sequence:

1. **Hero** — the story in one screen.
2. **Why** — why this exists.
3. **Problem** — the pressure point.
4. **Insight** — what was learned.
5. **Response** — what was built / changed.
6. **Offer** — services, products or tools.
7. **Proof** — evidence, case studies, trust.
8. **Action** — one obvious next step.

Sections can be suppressed when unnecessary, but not added simply to fill space.

## Visual rule

Every section must earn its place.

Use:
- strong documentary imagery
- one purposeful icon system
- functional colour
- concise cards
- diagrams when they explain a process faster than prose
- video where personality, process or trust benefits from motion

Avoid:
- walls of text
- endless same-size boxes
- decorative icon spam
- generic stock teams at laptops
- visual effects with no narrative purpose

## Definition of template success

A new website should be possible by changing the brief and approved assets, not rebuilding the application shell.

The system should populate:
- masthead
- page metadata
- story sections
- service cards
- imagery slots
- method marker where applicable
- free resources
- case studies
- trust
- CTAs
- commercial routes
- onboarding destinations
- footer/legal details

The website should feel bespoke because the **story, imagery and offer are bespoke**, while the underlying engineering remains controlled and reusable.
